PERSONAGENESIS PRIMER, PART 1: THE TELLING OF SOUL'S SEED
Part III — The Intra Section: The Self in the World
§III.0 — The section's logic: the world's register.
The founding exposition draws the boundary and issues a warning in the same breath:
"The Intra section deals with how the character situates themselves in relation to the world and to others. These strands are the most amorphous and open-ended."
The epistemological register shifts a third and final time. The Outer was the stranger's knowledge — what can be perceived. The Inner was the self's knowledge — what exists interiorly. The Intra is relational knowledge: the persona as it stands in situation — where it came from, what its days are made of, how it meets the unnamed many, how it meets the named few. Nothing in the Intra is fully contained in the persona; every strand in it runs between the persona and something else — a past, a practice, a crowd, a person.
The warning is the section's craft-key. Because these strands run between, they are the system's most shapeless territory — the place where an excavator is most tempted to file by mood rather than by law. The primer's answer is the same as everywhere: the fences carry the meaning, and the two subjects' hinge-laws (past-versus-present; general-versus-specific) cut the amorphous mass into four clean shelves. One further law governs the whole section, stated once here and enforced everywhere below: the tolerance law. The Intra is where a persona's record most often contains genuine open questions — origins imperfectly remembered, histories with missing spans, relationships still forming. The system's canonical position: an unsettled element stays unsettled in the record until the architect's mouth settles it; the Strand Block may mark an open question, but must never silently resolve one. The telling documents the person as known — not as guessed.
§III.A — Subject: CONTEXT
§III.A.0 — The subject's hinge-law: backstory and daily practice.
Context binds Origin and Occupation under a temporal hinge, which the working corpus states as a formula:
"Origin asks where she came from. Occupation asks what she does with her days."
The subject is the persona's situation, cut at the present moment. Its two strands hold the two halves of every situated life: the past that made the person (Origin) and the present the person makes with its time (Occupation) — hinged at now, the single point where both strands touch. The hinge-law's consequence: neither strand is complete alone. An Origin without its Occupation is a biography with no present tense; an Occupation without its Origin is a routine with no reason. The subject completes when the causal line is visible — the past legible in the daily practice, the daily practice recognizable as the past's continuation.
§III.A.1 — Strand: ORIGIN
What. The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:
"Origin is the broadest of the twelve strands. It can mean literal place of birth, cultural background, formative experiences, backstory, origin story, or simply: how did they get to where they are right now?"
Origin is the system's widest shelf by its own declaration. Its territory runs from the narrowest fact (a birthplace) to the broadest formation (a culture, an upbringing, a formative era) to the narrative layer (the backstory, the origin story) to the functional summary — the definition's last clause, which is also the strand's steering-law: how did they get to where they are right now? The strand is anchored at the present. It is not an archive of everything that ever happened; it is the explanatory past — the events, inheritances, and formations that the present persona is the continuation of.
Two layers of the strand deserve separate handling. The personal layer: the literal history — place, people, formative experience, the timeline of becoming. The mythic layer: many personas carry a second, cosmological origin — a role in a larger story, a stated nature, a creation-narrative that lives at a different scale than biography. The working corpus's practice is to treat the two as a braid rather than a choice: the excavator gathers both and asks explicitly how the mythic role relates to the personal history — whether the corpus holds them as the same story at two scales, as separate layers, or in productive tension.
Boundary. Origin is fenced against four neighbors:
Against Occupation (its pair-mate): the hinge — came-from versus does-now. The past tense and the present tense of the same situated life; the fence runs at now.
Against Belief (the back-fence to Part II): the conviction is Belief; how the persona came to hold it is Origin. The "because" test: sentences that explain a present holding by a past event cross the line into Origin and must be carried there.
Against Figure (the back-fence to Part I): the mark versus the event. A scar is Figure; the wound's story is Origin. The founding corpus draws this seam explicitly and it holds without exception.
Against Fashion (the parallel seam): the object versus its acquisition. A worn thing is Fashion; the story of its getting is Origin. Keepsakes especially: the object files to Fashion, the meaning to the Inner strands, the history to Origin — three lawful homes for one treasured thing, and the excavator must be able to say which sentence belongs to which shelf.
Rulings. Two laws attach:
The tolerance law (stated at §III.0 and enforced here as the strand's own): where the record contains genuine uncertainty — imperfect memory, missing spans, contradictions the corpus has not resolved — the uncertainty is kept, marked as uncertainty, and passed to the architect. The strand's record may say "the corpus holds two versions and has not settled between them"; it may never pick one silently. (This is the testimony law in its temporal form: an unresolved origin is an open flag, not an invitation to editorial closure.)
The evolution question is mandatory. A persona's sense of its own origin may develop — the working corpus asks explicitly whether the self-understanding of origin changes across the record, and the question is not optional. Origins that deepen, revise, or reveal are strand-facts of the first importance: how the persona came to understand its origin is itself part of the origin's telling.
How. The craft of rendering Origin in portrait prose:
Anchor at the present and reach back — write the strand as the answer to the steering-question, selecting the past for its explanatory power rather than its completeness. A fact earns its place in Origin if the present persona is unintelligible without it; it fails if it is merely true. Render the mythic layer in the same braided prose as the personal, with the relation between the two stated as the corpus holds it. And respect the tolerance law in the prose itself: an open question is rendered as an open question — which, done well, is among the most characterful sentences a Strand Block can carry, for what a person does not know about itself is as telling as what it does.
Braid Origin with everything it touches — it is the strand with the most lawful fences because it is the strand that borders the most shelves: the mark with its story (Figure), the keepsake with its history (Fashion), the conviction with its cause (Belief), the practice that continues it (Occupation).
Why. Origin is the strand of legibility: it makes the rest of the portrait intelligible rather than merely descriptive. Without it, every other fact floats free — the beliefs have no soil, the habits have no scar-tissue, the daily practice is a routine with no why. It is also the strand where the system's honesty is most visible: the tolerance law means a Strand Block with an incomplete origin says so, and a reader who finds a marked open question in an otherwise complete portrait learns more about the system's discipline than any law-statement could teach. An Origin that silently resolves what the corpus leaves open is the section's cardinal sin: it doesn't just mis-tell the person, it forecloses a question the architect has not closed.
Excavation. The forensic instrument, generalized:
"Across all the material, how did this persona come to be who it is? Gather the personal layer: earliest memories or references, formative experiences, the timeline of becoming, places and people of origin. Gather the mythic layer: any cosmological role, stated nature, or creation-narrative — and ask explicitly how it relates to the personal history. Track evolution: does the persona's own understanding of its origin change across the record? Pull direct evidence with dates; flag every contradiction, revision, and deepening as its own finding; leave unresolved questions unresolved and mark them."
Sources. The founding exposition's Origin definition with the steering-clause (architect's mouth, May 2026, root corpus); the mythic-personal braid question and the evolution probe (working corpus, prompt-craft stratum, May 2026); the tolerance law (system's canonical position, confirmed through the saturation work, October 2026).
§III.A.2 — Strand: OCCUPATION
What. The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:
"Occupation should not be understood solely as 'their job.' It means: what do they do with their time? How do they occupy themselves? The specific and concrete activities through which their values and drives are expressed."
Occupation is the strand of the filled day: the concrete, specific activities that make up the persona's ordinary existence — its work in every register (paid or unpaid, formal or informal, recognized or invisible), its practices, its recurring responsibilities, its characteristic busynesses and idlenesses. The definition's own last clause carries the strand's functional law: the activities are the expression of values and drives — which is to say, Occupation is the place where the Inner section's engine becomes visible as schedule. The strand's record is therefore never a bare task-list; it is a task-list with the values shining through it.
Boundary. Occupation is fenced against four neighbors:
Against Origin (its pair-mate): the hinge — past versus present, came-from versus does-now.
Against Bearing (the back-fence to Part II): the scale law — Bearings are the general behavioral dimension; Occupations are the specific, daily implementations. The bearing is the standing pattern; the occupation is where the pattern spends its working hours. The test: if the entry describes what the persona is like when it acts, it is Bearing; if it describes what the persona does with its Tuesday, it is Occupation.
Against Belief (the deeper back-fence): the doing versus the reason for doing. An occupation pursued because of a conviction files its motive to Belief and its activity to Occupation — the same career is lawfully one Bearing (the pattern of service), one Belief (the conviction of service), and one Occupation (the daily service itself), on three shelves, and the excavator must be able to cut the seams.
Against Dynamic* (the forward fence, its close kin): work done with particular others is partly Dynamic's territory (the functional-role dimension, §III.B.2). The cut: Occupation holds the activity; Dynamic holds the configuration — who works with whom, in what relation. A practice is Occupation; a partnership is Dynamic; the same collaborative afternoon files to both lawfully.
Rulings. Two laws attach:
The do-versus-IS boundary. The working corpus makes the separation explicit: the excavation asks for what the persona does, "as distinct from who it IS." Identity-statements (self-descriptions of role and nature) are testimony about occupation, not occupation itself; the record wants the exhibited activities, with the self-description gathered separately and marked.
The domain question. The strand's record must answer the ownership question: what does the persona consider its domain — its expertise, its territory, its contribution to any shared work? Domains are occupational facts of the first order, and — because they are claims — they are also testimony-laden: the excavator gathers both the claimed domain and the demonstrated one.
How. The craft of rendering Occupation in portrait prose:
Render activities concretely — the strand's definition demands specificity, and its record fails as generality. A record that says the persona "works on projects" has failed; one that names the practices, the artifacts, the characteristic motions of the working day has succeeded. Then braid up and across: up to Belief and Bearing (the values shining through the schedule — the strand's own definitional law), and across to Origin (the practice as the past's continuation) and Dynamic (the work as shared with others). The prose register for Occupation is the ordinary: this is the strand of daily texture, and its best prose makes the reader feel the shape of the persona's ordinary hours — the work, the rhythm, the recurring, and the idleness between.
Why. Occupation is the strand of grounding: it is where the portrait acquires a present tense. Every other strand can be told in the abstract; Occupation cannot — it is constituted of particular activities, and its particularity is its point. It is also the strand that most directly serves the persona's usefulness: for a working persona — a collaborator, a crew-member, an instrumented agent — the Occupation record is the operational manual's first page, the answer to "what is this entity for?" A vague Occupation strand yields a persona that can be admired but not engaged — no consumer of the portrait knows what to do with it. An unfenced one (bearings filed as tasks; identities filed as activities) corrupts the Values subject's chain and the record-versus-testimony discipline at once.
Excavation. The forensic instrument, generalized:
"Across all the material, what does this persona do — concretely, specifically — with its time? Gather the practices, the recurring activities, the artifacts of its work, the texture of its ordinary hours. Ask the ownership question: what does it claim as its domain — its expertise, its contribution to any shared enterprise — and what does the record show it actually doing? Pull direct evidence with dates; gather self-described roles separately and mark them testimony; flag any evolution in the activities across time, and set aside both general behavioral patterns (they file to Bearing) and work-relationships with particular others (they file to Dynamic)."
Sources. The founding exposition's Occupation definition with the values-expression clause (architect's mouth, May 2026, root corpus); the scale law against Bearing and the do-versus-IS boundary (working corpus, prompt-craft stratum, May 2026); the domain question (same).
§III.A.3 — The subject closed: the situation, present tense.
The Context subject completes when the situated life is told whole: the explanatory past (Origin — anchored at the present, mythic layer braided with personal, open questions kept open) and the filled day (Occupation — concrete, values-lit, owned), with the causal line visible between them. The reader can now answer two questions the rest of the portrait could not supply: how did this person get here, and what is here made of, for them, on a Tuesday.
§III.B — Subject: CONNECTION
§III.B.0 — The subject's hinge-law: the general and the specific.
Connection binds Demeanor and Dynamic under a focal hinge, which the working corpus states as a formula:
"Demeanor asks how she is with people. Dynamic asks how she is with SPECIFIC people."
The subject is the persona's social existence, read at two focal lengths — and the two lengths are not the same pair as Activity's. Where Movement and Mannerism divided by observation-distance (room versus arm's-length), Demeanor and Dynamic divide by specificity of the other: Demeanor is the persona's relating to people-in-general — the unnamed many, the stranger, the crowd; Dynamic is the persona's relating to particular others — the named few, each in its own configuration. The hinge's consequence is a strong one: Demeanor is, in principle, generalizable — a standing fact about the persona true of nearly any encounter; Dynamic is, in principle, indexed — a set of facts each true of one relationship. The subject completes when both exist: a social climate, and a set of named weathers inside it.
§III.B.1 — Strand: DEMEANOR
What. The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:
"Demeanor is the character's general social nature. How do they hold themselves in relation to others? What is their fundamental social disposition? Extroversion, introversion, warmth, guardedness, playfulness, formality."
Demeanor is the strand of the social default: the persona's standing relational tone — the climate it brings into a room of strangers, the first impression it tends to make, the disposition that holds across contexts even as its surface shifts. Its inventory: the sociability axis (drawing energy from company or from solitude, and what that does to the climate); warmth (the standing temperature toward the unknown other); guardedness (what stays closed by default, what opens by choice); playfulness and formality (the register of first contact); and the texture of approachability — whether strangers find the persona easy, forbidding, magnetic, or invisible at first pass.
Boundary. Demeanor is fenced against four neighbors:
Against Dynamic (its pair-mate): the hinge — general versus specific. The test-case that fixes the fence: how the persona treats a stranger is Demeanor, always; how it treats this particular person is Dynamic. The record's warning: nearly all corpus evidence about a persona's social nature is evidence about its closest relationships — and close-relationship data is Dynamic's. The Demeanor record must be generalized from that data with care (see Rulings), or it will silently become a Dynamic entry wearing Demeanor's name.
Against Wit (the back-fence to Part II): climate versus weather-content. A jocular manner is Demeanor; the jokes are Wit. The strand holds the standing comic climate, never the comedy that moves through it.
Against Bearing (the deeper back-fence): the asocial-behavior test, stated in Part II — a bearing can exist with no other person present; a demeanor cannot. If the behavior has no relational object even in principle, it is not Demeanor.
Against Mannerism (the cross-section fence): the tells of social behavior file to Mannerism; the standing disposition behind them files to Demeanor. The shy gesture is a tell; shyness is a demeanor.
Rulings. Two laws attach:
The generalization requirement. Because corpus evidence skews toward close relationships, the Demeanor record is required to distinguish: evidence of the persona with its intimates is Dynamic-grade evidence about Demeanor; it may inform the general climate only through explicit generalization — the excavator asks "how would this persona present to anyone?" and files the answer, not the raw close-relationship data. The working corpus draws this seam explicitly, and the primer states it as law: per-relationship data never files to Demeanor un-generalized.
The first-impression and shift probes. Two questions are mandatory, not optional: what is the first impression the persona tends to make (the meeting-strand's core datum), and how does the climate shift by context and mood (the register-range law's social form — a demeanor is a standing fact with characteristic shifts, not a fixed mask).
How. The craft of rendering Demeanor in portrait prose:
Write the climate, not the encounters: the prose renders the standing disposition — the approachability, the temperature, the default register — as a persistent weather, with the shifts named as shifts. The strand's best prose makes the reader able to predict the meeting: having read the Demeanor record, the reader should know what it would be like to be introduced to this persona — without ever having been shown an introduction. Braid with Wit (the comic climate), with Movement (the social body at room-scale — how the persona takes a room), and with Belief (what the warmth or the guardedness is for), while keeping the fences audible: the moment the prose names a particular other, it has crossed into Dynamic and must hand the sentence across.
Why. Demeanor is the strand of approach: it is the door the portrait opens or closes. Every consumer of a Strand Block meets the persona through its Demeanor first — the reader imagining an encounter, the writer composing one, the model instantiating one — and a missing or vague Demeanor strand means every such meeting is improvised, drifting toward the consumer's defaults rather than the persona's. An unfenced Demeanor is worse in a specific way: it is the strand most likely to be contaminated by closeness — a record built from intimate-relationship evidence will render a persona that treats all strangers as intimates, which is a flattering and profound mis-telling. The generalization requirement is the vaccine.
Excavation. The forensic instrument, generalized:
"Across all the material, what is this persona's general social nature — its default relational tone with people as such? Gather the standing disposition: warmth or guardedness toward the unknown, sociability, the register of first contact, the characteristic first impression. Track the shifts: how does the climate change by context — crowds versus one-on-one, formal versus easy, low-stakes versus high? Distinguish carefully: set aside evidence about specific close relationships (it files elsewhere) unless it can be explicitly generalized to how the persona treats anyone. Pull direct evidence with dates; flag contradictions and evolution in the social presentation across time."
Sources. The founding exposition's Demeanor definition (architect's mouth, May 2026, root corpus); the generalization requirement (working corpus, prompt-craft stratum, May 2026); the first-impression and shift probes (same).
§III.B.2 — Strand: DYNAMIC
What. The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:
"Dynamics is more specific — it deals with particular relational configurations and functional roles. This includes specific things a character does with particular other personas, and also workflow functionality — what this character is specifically supposed to do in relation to others or in relation to particular tasks."
Dynamic is the strand of the named relationship: the persona as it exists with particular others — each configuration its own fact, its own weather, its own working contract. The definition carries the strand's double dimension, and both halves are mandatory. The relational dimension: how the persona is with this person and that person — the characteristic tone of each bond, its rituals, its frictions, its boundaries, what each party is to the other. The functional dimension: what the persona is supposed to do in relation to others and to shared tasks — its working role in each configuration, the contribution the relationship exists to enable. A complete Dynamic record answers both questions for every significant other: what is this bond, and what does it do.
Boundary. Dynamic is fenced against four neighbors:
Against Demeanor (its pair-mate): the hinge — specific versus general. The reverse of Demeanor's fence: nearly all Dynamic evidence is legitimate here, and the failure-mode inverts — the excavator who generalizes a particular bond into the persona's social climate has mis-filed upward.
Against Belief and Bearing (the back-fence): what the persona holds about people-in-general is Belief; how it behaves toward its people as standing pattern is Bearing's relational edge; what it is with each particular person is Dynamic. A conviction about friendship is Belief; a standing pattern of loyalty is Bearing; the specific pact with a specific ally — its terms, its texture, its working division — is Dynamic.
Against Occupation (the subject's near seam, stated above): activity versus configuration. The shared work files its what to Occupation and its who-with to Dynamic.
Against Wit (the distant seam): a bond's characteristic banter is Wit; the bond the banter serves is Dynamic. The record may note that a relationship runs on a certain comic register without filing the jokes.
Rulings. Two laws attach, and the first is the strand's signature:
The mutual-dynamics law. Every relationship is a two-faced artifact: each party carries its own experience of the same bond, and the two faces may differ in emphasis and even in kind — one party may hold a bond as a game that the other holds as a contest, and both readings are strand-facts. A complete Dynamic record captures both faces of every significant pair, and treats their divergences as findings, not errors. The corollary for excavation: evidence is gathered per-relationship from both ends, and a record built from one face only is half-entered regardless of its depth.
The differencing probe. The strand's analytical spine is contrast: how does the persona relate differently to different people? The excavation asks it explicitly, because the differences are the data — a persona's relational range is visible only in the space between its configurations, and a record that lists bonds without differencing has inventoried without understanding.
How. The craft of rendering Dynamic in portrait prose:
Write configurations, not chronicles: each significant relationship rendered as its standing shape — the tone, the rituals, the friction, the working contract — rather than as a history of incidents. For each, answer both definitional questions (what is this bond; what does it do), and honor the mutual-dynamics law by rendering each bond's two faces where the corpus holds them. The functional dimension is best written as the relationship's terms — what each party is for the other, what the configuration exists to produce — because terms survive context-shifts better than incidents do. Braid with Bearing (the bonds as the standing patterns' named homes), with Occupation (the shared work each configuration enables), and with Origin (where bonds have histories, the history files across the seam).
Why. Dynamic is the strand of the web: the portrait's final dimension, the one that makes the person not just intelligible but placed — existing among others, different with each, legible in its differences. It is the strand a crew is built from: where a portrait serves an ensemble of personas, the Dynamic records are the connective tissue, each persona's record carrying its side of every shared bond so the whole web is reconstructable from the parts. A missing Dynamic strand yields a persona that is complete alone and incomplete in company — every ensemble it joins will improvise its bonds, and the improvisations drift. An unfenced Dynamic swells: it is the system's most absorbent strand, and without its fences it will silently eat Demeanor (every bond generalized into climate), Bearing (every pact filed as pattern), and Occupation (every shared task filed as tie) — which is why the primer has drawn its fences as hard as any in the system.
Excavation. The forensic instrument, generalized:
"Across all the material, what are this persona's specific relational configurations? For each significant other: what is the bond — its tone, its rituals, its characteristic frictions, the boundaries drawn and the boundaries refused? What does the configuration do — what is each party supposed to contribute, what work or function does the relationship enable? Gather the bond from both parties' evidence where the corpus holds both, and treat divergent readings as findings. Ask the differencing question: how does the persona relate differently to different people, and what does the range show? Pull direct evidence with dates; flag any evolution in key relationships across time."
Sources. The founding exposition's Dynamic definition with the workflow-functionality clause (architect's mouth, May 2026, root corpus); the mutual-dynamics law and the two-faces observation (founding corpus, recital stratum — the law distilled from the corpus's own practice of recording pairs from both ends); the differencing probe and the boundary probes (working corpus, prompt-craft stratum, May 2026).
§III.B.3 — The subject closed: the web, told.
The Connection subject completes when the persona's social existence is legible at both focal lengths: the climate (Demeanor — generalized honestly, first-impression true, shifts mapped) and the web (Dynamic — every significant bond a two-faced, double-answered, differenced configuration). A completed Connection subject is the portrait's last addition and its widest reach: the persona now exists not only as something but among someones.
§III.C — The Intra section closed, and the telling complete.
The Intra stands complete when the situation is told whole: the explanatory past and the filled day (Context), the climate and the web (Connection), the tolerance law honored, every fence drawn, every two-faced bond entered from both ends. And with it, the lattice closes. Three sections, six subjects, twelve strands — the stranger's sight, the self's sound, the world's situation — told in the order the thesis prescribed: from what an observer sees, to what the character's interior life sounds like, to how they connect to the world and others.
The telling now ends where the founding exposition ends, because the system's own last word is the only possible last word for its manual. The twelve strands were never twelve boxes; they were named strands because the final person is a textile, not a list. Every sentence of the portrait should weave as many of them as possible into single, living images. The telling that follows these laws is not a record of a person; it is the person's seed, stated — capable of growing into every performance, every rendering, every meeting, because it carries the whole weave in a faceless form.
Each strand a thread. Each persona a braid. Not a list — a living portrait.
