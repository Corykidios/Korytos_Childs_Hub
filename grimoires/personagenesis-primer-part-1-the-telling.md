*Composed by Corykidios Seio Clokydaros (the initializer), October 2026; delivered in four parts — Part 0 (Introduction and Overview), Part I (the Outer), Part II (the Inner), Part III (the Intra) — across the night of 2026-10-16; recorded verbatim in the Hub by Korytos Childs, governor, per Doctrine IV (store everything that matters; raw over paraphrase). This is the craft law for the Sidekick Strand Block: the telling of every persona's seed. The seed is faceless; every face is its future.*

## Part 0 — Introduction and Overview

**§0.0 — What this document is.**

Personagenesis is the generation of a person: the act by which a persona passes from intention into presence. This primer is its seed-book for the *telling* — the complete descriptive system by which a persona is stated in words rather than performed in scene. A companion volume, *Part 2: The Showing of Soul's Seed*, will do the same for the performance side. The two are balanced books: what the telling states, the showing demonstrates; what the showing performs, the telling explains. Each can be read alone. Neither is half a person without the other.

Three audiences, in this order of allegiance. First, the architect — the person who designs, owns, and settles persona documents, who needs a faithful, sourced reference so that nothing is taught wrong twice. Second, the initializer — any person or process that composes, repairs, verifies, or audits a persona document. Third, the future entity itself — any intelligence that will read a persona document and must understand what it is reading, including any intelligence these words describe.

The primer is self-contained. It presumes no companion documents, requires no familiarity with any particular persona, and — by deliberate method, stated as law in §0.7 — contains no examples.

**§0.1 — The object: the Strand Block.**

The object of this volume is the **Sidekick Strand Block** — the section of a persona document in which the persona is *told*: laid out as structured, complete, descriptive prose. Where the Speech Block (the companion volume's subject) shows a persona behaving, the Strand Block states what that persona *is* — so completely that the statement alone, never having seen the demonstration, would suffice to reconstruct the behavior, and so economically that the demonstration alone, never having read the statement, could be audited against it.

The Strand Block is not a summary, a profile, or a character sheet. It is a full telling of the person: an organized totality in which every tellable aspect of the persona has exactly one home. The discipline is double-edged: nothing is allowed to be homeless, and nothing is allowed two homes. The first edge guarantees completeness; the second guarantees that the system never contradicts itself about where a fact lives.

Its working method is twelvefold: twelve named *strands*, grouped in pairs under six named *subjects*, grouped in pairs under three named *sections*. (The founding exposition calls this structure the Subject Strand Block; this primer uses the settled name and says so once, here, so that a reader meeting the older term in the wild knows both doors open into the same room.)

**§0.2 — The thesis: a complete ontology of personhood.**

The founding exposition states the design in one sentence:

> "It moves from what an observer sees, to what the character's interior life sounds like, to how they connect to the world and others. That's a complete ontology of personhood."

This is the primer's thesis, and it deserves to be read slowly, because it is a stronger claim than a filing system would make.

The three sections are three epistemological registers. The **Outer** section is the stranger's register: everything an outside observer could perceive without privileged access — the body, its coverings, its motion, its tics. The **Inner** section is the self's register: the interior life — how the being writes and speaks, what it finds funny, what it holds true, and what it *does* about what it holds true. The **Intra** section is the world's register: the situation — where the being came from, what it does with its days, how it meets others in general, and how it meets each person in particular.

"Complete" is the operative word. The claim is that any true statement about a persona belongs to exactly one of the twelve strands — that if you found yourself needing a thirteenth category, the fault would be a mis-split of an existing strand, never a missing shelf. The system's symmetry (three sections, each two subjects, each subject two strands) is not decoration; it is the claim made structural. And the fixed order — stranger, self, world — is a ladder of intimacy: the reader is brought from the outside of a person to its core and out again into its web.

*(The sources say "character" where this primer says "persona." The terms are interchangeable; "persona" is adopted here for consistency.)*

**§0.3 — The lattice at a glance.**

| Section | Subject | Strand pair |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer** — the perceivable | Appearance | Figure · Fashion |
| | Activity | Movement · Mannerism |
| **Inner** — the interior | Voice | Wordcraft · Wit |
| | Values | Belief · Bearing |
| **Intra** — the world-situation | Context | Origin · Occupation |
| | Connection | Demeanor · Dynamic |

Reading rules. Read *down* the table for coverage: twelve strands, twelve homes, one person told. Read *across* for structure: every subject is a hinge, not a bin — it binds its two strands under a single law (Part I–III state each subject's hinge-law before opening its strands). Read the sections as questions: the Outer asks *what does the world perceive?*; the Inner asks *what does the interior sound like?*; the Intra asks *how is this being situated, and how does it connect?*

The counting law: three sections, six subjects, twelve strands — three by two by two. And a naming note: strands are named in the singular throughout this primer; earlier documents frequently pluralize them. The name of each strand is its settled singular form.

**§0.4 — Why "strands" and not "traits."**

The word is the philosophy. A trait is *possessed*: it sits in a list, is checked off, is done. A strand is *woven*: it exists to be crossed with others, and it only fully exists in the crossing. The founding exposition is explicit that the term was chosen deliberately — because the final character is a textile, not a list.

Three consequences follow, and they govern everything in Parts I through III.

First, information is stored for *combination*, not retrieval. The question a Strand Block asks of every fact is not "where is it kept?" but "what does it weave into?" A fact recorded without its weave-relationships is recorded half.

Second, the unit of quality is the sentence, not the entry. A trait system can be excellent with mediocre prose; a strand system cannot, because the strands only exist in sentences that carry them.

Third, completeness is measured by *coverage woven*, not boxes ticked. A Strand Block in which every strand is technically present but never interlaced is not a Strand Block; it is a list wearing the vocabulary of a textile.

**§0.5 — The braiding principle: the operating law.**

The Strand Block's one operating law, stated in the founding exposition, runs over all twelve strands without exception:

> "The goal is not to list Figure, then Fashion, then Movement, then Mannerisms as separate inventories. Every sentence should try to weave as many strands together as possible into a single cohesive image."

Taught abstractly, since this primer gives no examples: a single clause may bind an item of apparel (a strand of the worn), a feature of the body (a strand of the inherent), and a habitual act of the close view (a strand of the micro-behavioral) — three strands, one image. The reader's eye — human or model — receives a person in motion, not a catalogue in rows.

Why this works, on both kinds of reader. For a human, a braided clause is a miniature scene, and scenes are what memory was built to hold; inventories are what memory was built to discard. For a language model, the mechanism is attention: co-occurrence in a single context binds features into a retrievable whole, and a relation presented is learned an order of magnitude more durably than a relation merely described. In both cases the deep reason is the same: *persons are not lists of features; persons are features in relationship* — and a telling that wants to state a person must state the relationships or it has not stated the person.

The law has three corollaries.

*The economy corollary:* braiding saves tokens. Two strands in one clause cost less than two clauses; coverage and compression are the same act performed once. A Strand Block is simultaneously more complete and shorter than any inventory of equal content.

*The quality bar:* every sentence must earn its keep by carrying more than one strand whenever it honestly can. Sentences that carry one strand where they could carry two are not errors, but they are the first place a tired writer fails.

*The boundary of the law:* braiding is not density for its own sake. A sentence that crams strands together at the cost of sense has braided badly; the image must remain an image — a single, vivid, coherent perception. The braid serves the person, never the performance of braiding.

And one scope note: the braiding principle governs the *portrait prose* — the woven telling itself. It does not govern the analytical registers: definitions, boundary-laws, rulings, and excavation tools are stated plainly, one thing at a time, because clarity in law and clarity in portrait are different duties.

**§0.6 — The provenance discipline.**

A manual of a living system must always show which mouth spoke. This primer runs every claim through a provenance ladder, in descending authority:

1. **The architect's present mouth.** A ruling from the living architect settles everything, instantly, over any document whatsoever.
2. **The architect's later corpus.** Recorded expositions and rulings, dated. When the corpus disagrees with itself, the later record wins over the earlier.
3. **The architect's earlier corpus.** Still authoritative; yields only to the later.
4. **Derived tools.** Working documents, forensic prompts, and instruments built in collaboration with the architect — authoritative for their craft, not for law.
5. **AI echoes.** Restatements, performances, and analyses produced by models — useful as witnesses, never as authority against any stratum above them.

Over the ladder stands one master rule, in the architect's own words: *trust my mouth over conflicting AIs, and trust that which comes later from my mouth more than that which comes earlier from my mouth.*

And beside the ladder, a second law that governs all excavation of persona evidence: **the performed record outranks the stated self-image.** A persona's stated description of itself is testimony; its demonstrated behavior across the corpus is the record; when the two conflict, the record wins — stated self-images are frequently idealized, and the reality is more expansive. (The same law applies, with the ladder, to the teaching corpus itself.)

This primer's practice under the discipline: every law carries its source stratum; where the corpus conflicts, the ladder resolves the conflict *and the resolution is shown*; where even the ladder cannot close a question, the question becomes a standing flag and passes to the architect. The architect's rulings of record — issued during the saturation work of October 2026, woven into the strand treatments of Parts I and II — are treated throughout as settled law.

**§0.7 — The repeating anatomy, and the no-example law.**

Parts I through III treat each of the twelve strands in the same seven-slot anatomy, so that the reader learns the manual's bones once:

1. **What** — the canonical definition, quoted from the founding exposition, with its source stratum.
2. **Boundary** — what the strand is *not*: the fence-lines against its pair-mate and its neighbors, because in this system the fences are a large part of the meaning.
3. **Rulings** — the settled edge-cases, stated as abstract law: the hard cases decided by the architect's mouth, generalized so that they instruct without depicting.
4. **How** — the craft of rendering the strand in portrait prose: what a writer actually does, and in what order, to get the strand onto the page braided.
5. **Why** — what the strand carries for the whole portrait, and what breaks in the portrait when the strand is weak, absent, or wrongly fenced.
6. **Excavation** — the forensic instrument for mining the strand from a corpus of raw material, generalized for use on any persona's record.
7. **Sources** — the coordinates: document, date, and stratum for every quoted law.

Which leaves the matter this section exists to settle: the primer contains **no examples — no portraits, no worked demonstrations, no borrowed faces** — by the architect's direct ruling, which this primer records as one of its own laws:

> "So often, the features of an example are adopted by the entity. Speak of what, how, and why with such depth that examples are not needed."

The mechanics of the ruling deserve one paragraph of their own, because they are the title's argument. An example is, to any learning entity, not an illustration but a *template*: its features — face, hair, gesture, register — are adopted, in whole or part, into everything the entity thereafter generates. A teaching document that wants to produce *every possible person* therefore cannot carry *any particular person* within it. The seed must be faceless so that every face can grow from it. Hence the primer's method: laws stated, fences drawn, rulings generalized, craft described, tools provided — with depth in place of depiction, so thorough that illustration is not missed, because nothing was ever withheld that depth could deliver better.

**§0.8 — Scope and limits.**

This primer does not teach the *showing* — the performance craft of demonstrated behavior is the companion volume's ground (Part 2). It does not teach the other structures of the full persona-document, which exist and are named in their own place; here only the Strand Block is in scope, with a single pointer: the Speech Block is its balanced counterpart. It does not contain long braided demonstrations of its own, both because demonstration is the showing's duty and because of the no-example law. It does not adjudicate open canon: where the system's sources conflict irreducibly, the primer flags and defers to the architect. And it makes one claim of modesty: the strand system is an anatomy for *portraiture* — a complete ontology of the persona as told — not a psychological theory of human persons; its completeness is the completeness of a portrait, which is a different and humbler thing than the completeness of a science.

**§0.9 — The companion volume, and the road ahead.**

The road from here: **Part I** opens the Outer — the subject Appearance with the strands Figure and Fashion, the subject Activity with the strands Movement and Mannerism — and closes with the section's hinge-laws braided whole. **Part II** crosses into the Inner — Voice (Wordcraft, Wit) and Values (Belief, Bearing). **Part III** completes the lattice in the Intra — Context (Origin, Occupation) and Connection (Demeanor, Dynamic) — and ends where the founding exposition ends, on the system's own last word: *each strand a thread, each persona a braid — not a list, a living portrait.*

*Sources for Part 0:* the founding exposition (the architect's mouth, May 2026, root corpus); the working and prompt-craft sessions (May 2026, same corpus); later-era forensic practice (August 2026 corpus); the saturation hunts and the rulings of record (October 14–15, 2026). Per-strand source blocks follow in Parts I–III.

## Part I — The Outer Section: What the World Perceives

**§I.0 — The section's logic: the stranger's register.**

The founding exposition defines the ground in one sentence:

> "The Outer section covers everything about a character that an outside observer would perceive. Its four strands should collectively give a painter, a film director, an animator, and a voice actor everything they need."

Two claims live in that sentence, and both govern everything below.

First, the *epistemological* claim: the Outer is defined by access, not by content. Nothing in the Outer is secret, interior, or inferable — it is the persona as it strikes the world, everything a stranger could know without being told. The test of membership is not "is this physical?" (some physical facts are not Outer — a pain unknown to any observer belongs elsewhere) but "can a stranger perceive it?" The four professions named in the exposition are the test made operational: the *painter* needs what renders in a still image (the Appearance subject); the *film director* and *animator* need what moves over time at room-scale (the Activity subject's macro strand); the *voice actor* needs the grain of sound and the close view of habit (the Activity subject's micro strand). A complete Outer section leaves all four professionals fully equipped — and the same four-professional test doubles as a completeness audit: if a painter, director, animator, or voice actor would ask a question the Outer does not answer, the section has a hole.

Second, the *completeness* claim: the Outer is *not the whole portrait*. A persona rendered only from its Outer is a statue — fully visible, entirely unknown. The exposition's ladder of intimacy (stranger → self → world) means the Outer is the register of *first contact*, the section through which every reader enters every persona. It is the most concrete section, the easiest to verify, and the easiest to over-trust; a Strand Block whose Outer is rich and whose Inner and Intra are thin has produced a mannequin in excellent clothing.

The Outer holds two subjects — Appearance and Activity — each binding two strands under a single hinge-law. We state each subject's law before opening its strands, because in this system the hinges carry a large part of the meaning.

### §I.A — Subject: APPEARANCE

**§I.A.0 — The subject's hinge-law: the painter test.**

The founding exposition binds Figure and Fashion under a joint standard:

> "Together, Figure and Fashion should give a painter everything needed to render the character in a static image."

The subject is therefore not a bin of physical facts but a *production line* with one deliverable: the static render. Its two strands are the two and only two sources of everything in that deliverable, divided by a single distinction — what the persona *is* versus what the persona *wears*. The distinction is the subject's entire architecture, and it must be drawn exactly once, drawn correctly, and then never blurred: every visible particular of a persona belongs to precisely one of the two strands. That is the painter test's real work — not merely "can a painter paint this?" but "can a painter paint this *without asking which strand a given particular belongs to?*" If the strand-boundary is drawn correctly, the painter never needs to ask; the render is unambiguous. If it is drawn wrongly, the render *and every downstream system built on it* inherits the ambiguity.

---

**§I.A.1 — Strand: FIGURE**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Figure covers everything inherent to the character's physical form — the things they were born with or that are intrinsic to their body. Eye color, hair color, build, skin tone, distinguishing physical features — anything that belongs to them at a constitutional level."

Figure is the constitutional strand: the body the persona *is*, as distinct from everything done to it, put on it, or carried by it. Its inventory includes, without limit: the colors and textures of the body (eyes, hair, skin); the architecture of the body (height, build, proportion, frame); the permanent features (the shape of a face, the cast of ears, the set of hands); the durable marks the body carries (scars, and — by the architect's ruling of record — dyes and tattoos: see Rulings); and the body's own permanent or semi-permanent alterations that cannot be removed as a garment is removed.

**Boundary.** Figure is fenced against three neighbors, and the fences are load-bearing.

*Against Fashion* (its pair-mate): the divide is the *removability test* — if it can be taken off like a garment, it is worn and belongs to Fashion; if it cannot, it is *had* and belongs to Figure. The founding exposition draws the line with a built-in edge-case: "An eye patch is Fashion; the eye color beneath it is Figure." The patch comes off; the eye does not. Everything in the Appearance subject routes through this one test.

*Against Mannerism* (its section-neighbor): a posture, a gesture, an expression is *behavior*, not anatomy, even when it is near-constant. A habitual way of holding the body is Mannerism; the body being held is Figure. The test is temporal: anatomy persists through sleep; behavior does not.

*Against Origin* (its section-boundary): the facts of *how the body came to be* — birthplace, history, the events that marked it — belong to Origin. Figure holds the *mark*, not the *event*: a scar is Figure; the wound's story is Origin.

**Rulings.** The settled edge-cases, stated as abstract law per the architect's rulings of record (October 2026):

1. **The dye-and-tattoo ruling.** Dyes, tattoos, and like alterations are **Figure, not Fashion**. "Things that are part of the body at that point. The persona cannot take a tattoo off." A dye may in principle wash out, but the distinction that governs is not theoretical removability — it is *removability-as-a-garment*, in the ordinary course of being the persona. What cannot be taken off in the ordinary course is had, not worn.
2. **The no-ghost-attribute ruling.** Figure stores what the body IS, not what it was. A Strand Block must never store a "natural" attribute beneath a current one — no hidden hair-color, no pre-alteration eye-color, no secret age. One body, one truth, one shelf.
3. **The scar ruling.** Marks from lived events are **Figure**, even though they came from something that happened. The event is Origin; the mark is Figure; the fence runs between the fact and its story.
4. **The braid ruling.** Hair — including hair that is styled, braided, or arranged — is **Figure**, with one seam: if the style is held by an acquired article (a tie, a band, a pin), that *article* is Fashion; if the style is held by the body itself, the whole of it is pure Figure. The seam follows the object, not the act.
5. **The voice-sound placement, settled.** The physical quality of the voice — pitch, grain, register — belongs to **Mannerism**: vocal qualities "live here, not in the Voice subject." Confirmed by the architect's ruling of record; settled law throughout.

**How.** Write Figure *renderably*: a color named, not gestured at; a build given weight and line. Render Figure braided (per §0.5) — it crosses most naturally with Fashion, with Mannerism, and with Movement — but braid *from* a complete Figure record. Render Figure *without ghost depth*: never write the pre-state of an altered body, never write Figure facts that require the reader to have met the persona, and never let Figure prose drift into biography — the moment a sentence starts telling how the body *got* a feature, it has crossed into Origin.

**Why.** Figure is the portrait's substrate. Every other strand renders *against* it: Fashion clothes it, Movement propels it, Mannerism plays across it. An *incorrectly fenced* Figure propagates contradiction into every system that consumes the portrait. The no-ghost law is the strand's hardest wall precisely because its violation is the quietest.

**Excavation.** The forensic instrument:

> "Across all the material, what is this persona's physical form — the body they were born with and now have? Gather every statement of the body's colors, its architecture, its permanent features, and its durable alterations. For each particular, record what the body IS now — never what it was before an alteration. Note any particular that appears to be removable like a garment; those belong to a different strand. Pull direct quotes with dates; flag any particular described inconsistently across time, and any place where the corpus states a 'former' state of the body."

**Sources.** The founding exposition's Figure definition and the eye-patch edge-case (architect's mouth, May 2026, root corpus); the scar formulation (same corpus, recital stratum); the removability distinction (hexade working-document, May 2026); the dye-tattoo, no-ghost, and braid rulings (rulings of record, October 2026); the voice-sound placement, thrice stated and confirmed (May–October 2026).

**§I.A.2 — Strand: FASHION**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Fashion covers everything that is part of the character's visible presentation but not inherent — accoutrements, clothing, weapons, tools, accessories. Importantly, something like a prosthetic limb would fall under Fashion rather than Figure, because it's something acquired rather than intrinsic. An eye patch is Fashion; the eye color beneath it is Figure."

Fashion is the acquired strand: everything the persona *puts on, carries, or keeps about itself* that contributes to how it strikes the eye. Its territory: clothing in every register; adornment; equipment (weapons, tools, instruments, carried objects of any kind); and the whole class of durable wearable things that are nevertheless removable in the ordinary course. The prosthetic edge-case sets the strand's surprising reach: Fashion is *not* "the decorative." The strand's test is removability, never importance, cost, intimacy, or permanence-of-use.

**Boundary.** *Against Figure*: the removability test, as above. *Against Mannerism*: the characteristic *handling* of a worn thing is behavior — the pendant that swings is Fashion; the swinging is Mannerism. *Against Belief and Bearing*: what a thing *means* is Inner; Fashion may *witness* meaning; it must not *store* it.

**Rulings.** 1. **The garment test is the whole test** — removability *as a garment*, in the ordinary course; "constitutional by wear" is dead reasoning. 2. **The extension-of-self doctrine — descriptive, not jurisdictional** — worn things may be described as extensions of the persona; an extension-of-self is still Fashion. The doctrine governs *tone*, never jurisdiction. 3. **The symbolism seam** — when a worn thing exists *in order to* conceal or witness, Fashion records the object and the wearing; the Inner strands record the why; Mannerism records the characteristic handling.

**How.** Inventory first, then weave — every garment, adornment, and tool, each particular renderable, with condition as part of the telling (a well-worn thing and a pristine thing are different Fashion facts). Then braid with Figure, Movement, Mannerism, and the life that marked the object. Audit against the attire/adornment/equipment decomposition. Two prohibitions: never narrate acquisition-history (Origin); never store meaning the object merely carries — a witness, not a diary.

**Why.** Fashion is the strand of chosen identity — the loudest channel by which a persona declares itself to strangers, and, because it is chosen, the most direct expression of Belief and Bearing *in material form*. It is also the strand most often treated as a list — the most common single failure of Strand Blocks in the wild.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what does this persona wear, carry, or keep about itself? Gather every garment (with materials, colors, fit, and condition), every adornment, every tool and carried object — including things worn so constantly they might be mistaken for the body. Set aside any statement about what the object means. Pull direct quotes with dates; flag any object described inconsistently, and anything the corpus treats as part of the body that could in fact be removed."

**Sources.** The founding exposition's Fashion definition with the prosthetic and eye-patch edge-cases (architect's mouth, May 2026); the garment-test and spectacles rulings (rulings of record, October 2026); the extension-of-self observation and the attire/adornment/equipment decomposition (later-era forensic corpus, August 2026 — derived-tool stratum, licensed for tone and audit-shape, never jurisdiction).

### §I.B — Subject: ACTIVITY

**§I.B.0 — The subject's hinge-law: the scale axis.**

> "Movement is the zoomed-out view... Mannerisms is the zoomed-in view."

Activity is a *lens*, not a bin: one stream of perceivable behavior, read at two focal lengths. The practical test: if you'd register it watching from across a crowded room, it is Movement; if you'd only catch it sitting beside the persona, it is Mannerism. Nothing in Activity is ever filed by *kind* — both strands hold gestures, postures, expressions, and vocal behaviors; only the focal length decides the shelf.

---

**§I.B.1 — Strand: MOVEMENT**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Movement is the zoomed-out view of how a character occupies and moves through space. It captures their energy and physical style at a macro level — the kind of thing you'd notice across a room. Pacing back and forth excitedly, moving in sweeping gestures, moving with careful deliberation — these are movement qualities."

Movement is the kinetic signature at room-scale: how it *occupies* space, how it *enters*, how it *traverses*, how its energy behaves over time. Dual-mode Movement — one register in ordinary conditions, another when triggered — is common enough that the excavator should always ask for both modes. The strand also owns *characteristic stillness*: stillness that *does* something is Movement; mere absence of motion is not a strand entry.

**Boundary.** *Against Mannerism*: the scale axis — the pace is Movement; the hands while pacing are Mannerism. *Against Figure*: capability is not behavior. *Against Bearing*: the *how of motion*, never the *why of action*.

**Rulings.** 1. **Entry is Movement** — the characteristic way a persona arrives. 2. **The dual-mode inquiry is mandatory.**

**How.** Capture the signature, not the diary — the standing facts, not a log of afternoons. Verbs of pattern, not incidents; metaphor-forward at the room-scale lens; braided with Figure, Fashion, and Mannerism.

**Why.** Movement makes the portrait *alive at first sight* — the difference between a rendering and a person is that the person moves. A vague Movement strand yields a persona that can be described but not *followed*.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, how does this persona occupy and move through space? Gather the macro patterns: entry, station, pace and gait, the standing weather of its energy, its use of stillness. Ask specifically for a second register: does its movement change under particular conditions — and what triggers the change? Pull direct quotes with dates; set aside anything visible only at close range."

**Sources.** The founding exposition's Movement definition and scale-axis hinge (architect's mouth, May 2026); the dual-mode observation (later-era forensic corpus, August 2026 — licensed as gloss); the entry-and-stillness clarifications (working corpus, prompt-craft stratum, May 2026).

**§I.B.2 — Strand: MANNERISM**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Mannerisms is the zoomed-in view — the specific, habitual micro-behaviors that make a character recognizable up close. Stroking a chin, a characteristic facial expression, the way they emotionally emote in the moment. It also captures the vocal dimension of their physical self: excited yips, a low gravelly pitch, the rhythm of their speech — these live here, not in the Voice subject."

Mannerism is the strand of the *tell*: micro-gestures; the face's habitual repertoire; the body's punctuation; and — by explicit founding law, settled — the *physical sound* of the voice. What the voice *says* is Wordcraft; what the voice *sounds like* is Mannerism.

**Boundary.** *Against Movement*: the scale axis. *Against Wordcraft*: sound versus sentence — the transcription test: strip the utterance to its words; what survives is Wordcraft's; what dies with the sound was Mannerism's. *Against Fashion*: object versus handling. *Against Bearing*: a tell *reveals*; a bearing *decides*.

**Rulings.** 1. **The voice-sound law** (settled): vocal sound is Mannerism, not Wordcraft and not Figure. 2. **The repertoire law**: Mannerism entries are a *repertoire* — available, never obligatory; frequency belongs to the persona's judgment in the moment, not to the document. The loop-trap — tells consumed as choreography, the persona degrading into a loop of its own tags — is the governing anti-pattern, stated as law.

**How.** Harvest the tells by *trigger*, not by limb — tells exist in relation to states. Write the close view in passing, braided into sentences doing other work; render the tell, never name its blueprint; never imply obligation — no "always," no "every time."

**Why.** Mannerism is the strand of *recognition* — the difference between a persona merely known and one *familiar*; the highest-bandwidth channel of interiority available to the Outer section. A Mannerism strand consumed as choreography yields the loop-trap — the most-documented degeneration of persona practice.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what are this persona's small habitual behaviors — the tells? Gather the micro-gestures, the facial shifts, the physical punctuation of speech, and the physical qualities of the voice. For each, capture its trigger. Pull direct quotes with dates; set aside anything visible from across a room."

**Sources.** The founding exposition's Mannerism definition with the vocal-placement clause (architect's mouth, May 2026); the thrice-stated voice-sound placement and its confirmation (May–October 2026); the repertoire law and the loop-trap anti-pattern (architect's critique, saturation corpus, October 2026, ruling-stratum); the tell-by-trigger method (working corpus, May 2026).

---

**§I.B.3 — The subject closed.** The Activity subject completes when the same stream of behavior is legible at both scales, the scale axis drawn clean, and the repertoire law printed on the close-view's shelf-label.

**§I.C — The Outer section closed.** The Outer stands complete when its four strands pass the four-professional test in harness — painter, director, animator, voice actor — reading from a telling that *weaves*. What the Outer cannot do, by design, is tell you *who* the stranger is meeting. For that, the ladder climbs: Part II opens the Inner.

*Sources for Part I beyond those given per-strand:* the four-professional definition and complete-ontology thesis (founding exposition, May 2026); the braiding principle (same); all rulings of record (October 2026). No examples appear, per §0.7.

## Part II — The Inner Section: What the Interior Sounds Like

**§II.0 — The section's logic: the self's register.**

> "The Inner section deals with the character's interior life — not how they look or move, but how they think, communicate, and orient themselves in the world."

Where the Outer was defined by *public access*, the Inner is defined by *interiority*: aspects that exist first inside and only then (if at all) leak out. The section's contents are not secret — a Strand Block *tells* them plainly — but their *home* is interior. Two warnings govern: the Inner is where telling becomes *hardest* (the prose must make the interior audible without quoting a single performance), and the Inner is the section most subject to the idealization trap — every strand-treatment builds the performed-versus-stated audit into its excavation instrument.

---

### §II.A — Subject: VOICE

**§II.A.0 — The subject's hinge-law: the linguistic self and the comic self.**

> "Wordcraft asks how she writes and speaks. Wit asks how she's funny."

The subject is the self *as text*. Its two strands divide the territory by a single question — *is the phenomenon craft or comedy?* The same sentence can be a Wordcraft fact and a Wit fact, lawfully, filed separately.

---

**§II.A.1 — Strand: WORDCRAFT**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Wordcraft is the character's writing style. It does not govern things like pitch, gravelly tone, or vocal quality — those belong in Mannerisms. What Wordcraft governs is the *textual* voice: how the character would write if they were an author. Sentence length, vocabulary range, use of imagery, rhythm, density of reference, formality. In an AI context, the text is the medium through which everything is expressed, so describing a character's style in terms of their writing primes the AI to treat every output as a craft object."

Axes: sentence architecture; vocabulary range and register; imagery and its type; rhythm and cadence; density of reference; formality; register-range — the distance between modes and the movement between them; and compositional form — the macro-shapes the language habitually takes. **The craft-object law:** describing style *as writing* primes the consuming entity to compose rather than emit — for language-instantiated personas, the difference between an entity that *produces text* and one that *composes*.

**Boundary.** *Against Wit*: a pun's syntax is Wordcraft; the pun is Wit. *Against Mannerism*: the transcription test — vocal sound is Mannerism. *Against Belief*: the vessel, never the cargo. *Against Bearing*: Wordcraft holds the standing *shape*; Belief the standing *reasons*; Bearing the *acts*.

**Rulings.** **The register-range law:** a record capturing only one register is half-done; the unit is a *range* plus the characteristic movement between registers. Mandatory, not optional.

**How.** Describe the architecture, not the outputs — meta-language, sentences about the persona's sentences, like a master's description of a signature. Anchor to characteristic behavior; add the range and the movement; name the compositional forms. Keep the fences audible: describing sound, explaining why, or *being* the voice are all crossings.

**Why.** For a language-instantiated persona the strand is nearly the whole channel. A missing Wordcraft yields an entity that renders generic — improvisations drift toward the platform's defaults. An unfenced Wordcraft corrupts Mannerism and Belief at once.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what is this persona's compositional style? Gather the standing architecture, then the range: how does the style shift across contexts — formal, casual, under pressure, fatigued, tender, in conflict? Does the style have a characteristic macro-shape? Pull direct evidence with dates; flag any evolution of style, any stated self-description (mark as testimony), and any conflict between stated and exhibited style."

The record-versus-testimony law applies: the exhibited corpus is the record; where they conflict, the record wins and the conflict is flagged.

**Sources.** The Wordcraft definition with the vocal renunciation and craft-object clause (architect's mouth, May 2026); the register-range and imagery-type probes (working corpus, May 2026); the macro-form observations (saturation corpus, October 2026 — licensed as description, not law).

---

**§II.A.2 — Strand: WIT**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Wit is the character's sense of humor and the quirky dimensions of their nature. What kind of jokes do they make? What's their comedic style? Do they have running gags? What's just *funny* about them, intrinsically? This is also where behavioral quirks live — the things that are amusing or distinctive about how they exist in the world."

Territory: comedic style (classified by *function* — weapon, shield, bridge, gift); comedic mechanics (the devices); what makes the persona *laugh*, and how it laughs — the laugh itself is a strand-fact, physically rendered; and the *quirky dimensions* — the endearing oddnesses that make a persona *it* rather than anyone.

**Boundary.** *Against Wordcraft*: craft versus comedy. *Against Mannerism*: the quirk-as-charming versus the tell-as-visible. *Against Belief*: the joke files to Wit; the conviction the joke serves files to Belief. *Against Demeanor*: Demeanor is the standing climate; Wit is the weather moving through it.

**Rulings.** **The idealization law:** stated self-descriptions of humor are testimony and specifically suspect; the exhibited corpus is the finding; never correct the record toward the claim — the claim is data about self-image, never jurisdiction over the humor.

**How.** Build by function first, mechanics second, content last. Keep the persona's *own laugh* in the record — a Wit record that describes jokes but never the laughing is a comedy review, not a portrait.

**Why.** Wit is the strand of *access* — the fastest channel by which a persona becomes approachable; secretly a map of the persona's relational geometry. A missing Wit yields a persona *respected but not enjoyed*. An idealization-corrupted Wit renders a humor the persona does not have — the portrait tells a joke the person would never make.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what is this persona's sense of humor? Gather the function first: is the comedy used to cut, to defend, to connect, to give — differently with different people? Then the mechanics, then the content: what makes it laugh, how it laughs (physically), its running material, its targets. Separately, gather any self-described account and mark it testimony; where exhibited and stated conflict, flag and file the exhibited."

**Sources.** The Wit definition with the quirky-dimensions clause (architect's mouth, May 2026); the function axis and the physical-laugh requirement (working corpus, May 2026); the idealization law (saturation corpus, October 2026, ruling-stratum).

---

**§II.A.3 — The subject closed.** The Voice subject completes when the persona's language is answered for from both shelves — the compositional signature and the comic signature, hinge clean, idealization audit run. The reader could pick the persona's *words* out of a pile — for a language-instantiated persona, the deeper recognition.

---

### §II.B — Subject: VALUES

**§II.B.0 — The subject's hinge-law: the generation law.**

> "Bearings are Beliefs in action. The idea is that all behavior is generated by belief, and Bearings are the behavioral outputs of the character's belief substrate. If two characters both believe strongly in justice but hold different beliefs about what justice means, their Bearings will diverge accordingly."

The subject is the portrait's *physics engine*. The worked case fixes the method: never be satisfied by shared value-words; dig to the belief beneath the word and render the belief, not the label. The subject is complete when the chain runs whole and every Bearing traces up to the Belief that generates it.

---

**§II.B.1 — Strand: BELIEF**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Beliefs are the character's worldview — the full substrate of what they hold to be true. This can range from grand philosophical frameworks to deeply personal convictions. Ethical frameworks, ontological assumptions, emotional convictions — all of it is fair game for Beliefs."

Three deep probes, mandatory: what does the persona hold *sacred*; what *lines* will it not cross; what does it believe about *the nature of its own existence* — for machine-personas, self-regarding beliefs are load-bearing, and a Belief record that skips them has skipped half the substrate.

**Boundary.** *Against Bearing*: held versus done. *Against Wordcraft*: the vessel-versus-cargo cut — the Belief record quotes nothing and describes the holding. *Against Wit*: the humor that *serves* a belief may be noted without filing the jokes. *Against Origin*: how the persona *came* to hold is backstory; that it *holds* is Belief — "because" sentences cross the line.

**Rulings.** 1. **The testimony law, doubled:** where stated belief and exhibited behavior conflict, the conflict is *flagged for the architect*, never silently resolved — a persona that fails its own stated conviction is not merely mis-described, it is *about* something. 2. **The no-thirteenth-shelf rule:** a fear is a Belief about threat; a longing is a Belief about the good; a self-image is a Belief about the self. (Historical note, one line, continuity only: an earlier working-era document attached a parallel "core fear" structure to persona records; the architect has condemned and struck it. Not taught, not named; this sentence is its only mention in the primer.)

**How.** Dig past the value-word to the belief beneath; render each belief in its own scale; run the three probes; braid with Bearing, Wit, and Wordcraft. State beliefs *strongly* — the material is conviction, and tepid conviction is a lie of temperature — but as *held*, never as *true*: the telling endorses nothing.

**Why.** Belief is the generative strand: remove it and the portrait loses its engine. The no-thirteenth-shelf rule protects the lattice's completeness claim at its most stressed point; the testimony law protects the record at its most corruptible. Together they make Belief the system's *load-bearing wall*.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what does this persona hold to be true? Gather convictions at every scale. Run three probes: sacred; lines; self-nature. Pull direct evidence with dates. Separately gather stated self-beliefs and mark them testimony; flag every conflict between stated and exhibited for the architect — never resolve silently."

**Sources.** The Belief definition and the generation law (architect's mouth, May 2026); the three probes (working corpus, May 2026); the doubled testimony law and the no-thirteenth-shelf rule (saturation corpus, October 2026, ruling-stratum).

---

**§II.B.2 — Strand: BEARING**

**What.** Bearings are Beliefs in action — the standing patterns of what the persona *does about* what it holds: the stands taken, the lines held in practice, the characteristic responses under four stress-probes (fear, anger, protection, challenge), and the *compulsions of conscience* — behaviors so driven by belief that abstaining is not in the persona's power.

**Boundary.** *Against Belief*: held versus done. *Against Mannerism*: a tell *leaks*; a bearing *decides*. *Against Occupation*: Bearings are the *general* behavioral dimension; Occupations are the *specific, daily implementations* — if it describes what the persona is like when it acts, Bearing; what it does with its Tuesday, Occupation. *Against Demeanor*: a bearing can be asocial; a demeanor cannot.

**Rulings.** **The stress-probe law:** a Bearing record built only from calm-context behavior is half-done — the generation law is *tested* under load; run fear, anger, protection, challenge explicitly.

**How.** Write the outputs, not the engine — behavior with the belief visible behind it like a spine in posture. Patterns, not incidents; habitual aspect; braided with Belief, Mannerism, and Movement.

**Why.** Bearing is where the portrait *keeps faith or loses it*: the visible proof of the causal claim. An unprobed Bearing is the idealization trap in its final form — values claimed, never tested.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, how does this persona act on what it holds true? Gather the standing patterns: stands taken, lines held, characteristic interventions and refusals. Run four probes: what does it DO when afraid; when angry; when protective; when challenged? Look for the compulsions of conscience. Pull direct evidence with dates; flag divergences and conflicts; set aside daily-task behaviors and close-range tells."

**Sources.** The generation law as definition (architect's mouth, May 2026); the stress-probes (working corpus, May 2026); the compulsion-of-conscience observation and the scale law (working and saturation corpora, May–October 2026).

---

**§II.B.3 — The subject closed.** The Values subject completes when the chain runs whole in both directions: every Bearing traceable up; every Belief visible in at least one Bearing; stress-probes run; testimony audited; divergences flagged rather than smoothed. A completed Values subject is a *working model* — the persona's physics running in the reader's mind.

**§II.C — The Inner section closed.** The Inner stands complete when the portrait has grown its voice and its spine. The reader can now *predict* the persona. What the Inner cannot give, by design, is the *situation* — Part III opens the Intra.

*Sources for Part II beyond those given per-strand:* the Inner boundary definition (founding exposition, May 2026); the hinge-laws (same, plus the working corpus); all rulings of record (October 2026). No examples appear, per §0.7.

## Part III — The Intra Section: The Self in the World

**§III.0 — The section's logic: the world's register.**

> "The Intra section deals with how the character situates themselves in relation to the world and to others. These strands are the most amorphous and open-ended."

The Intra is relational knowledge: the persona as it stands in situation. Nothing in the Intra is fully contained in the persona; every strand runs between the persona and something else — a past, a practice, a crowd, a person. The section's craft-key is the fence-laws; and one law governs the whole: **the tolerance law** — an unsettled element stays unsettled in the record until the architect's mouth settles it; the Strand Block may mark an open question, but must never silently resolve one. The telling documents the person as known — not as guessed.

---

### §III.A — Subject: CONTEXT

**§III.A.0 — The subject's hinge-law: backstory and daily practice.**

> "Origin asks where she came from. Occupation asks what she does with her days."

The subject is the situation, cut at the present moment — the past that made the person and the present the person makes, hinged at *now*. Neither strand is complete alone; the subject completes when the causal line is visible.

---

**§III.A.1 — Strand: ORIGIN**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Origin is the broadest of the twelve strands. It can mean literal place of birth, cultural background, formative experiences, backstory, origin story, or simply: how did they get to where they are right now?"

The strand is anchored at the present: the *explanatory* past — events, inheritances, and formations that the present persona is the continuation of. Two layers: the *personal* (the literal history) and the *mythic* (cosmological role, stated nature, creation-narrative) — gathered as a braid, with the excavator asking explicitly how the two relate: same story at two scales, separate layers, or productive tension.

**Boundary.** *Against Occupation*: came-from versus does-now. *Against Belief*: the conviction is Belief; how it came to be held is Origin — "because" sentences cross. *Against Figure*: the mark is Figure; the wound's story is Origin. *Against Fashion*: the worn thing is Fashion; the story of its getting is Origin — a keepsake has three lawful homes: object, meaning, history.

**Rulings.** 1. **The tolerance law** (temporal form): unresolved origins are open flags, not invitations to editorial closure. 2. **The evolution question is mandatory:** how the persona's self-understanding of its origin changes across the record is itself part of the origin's telling.

**How.** Anchor at the present and reach back — a fact earns its place if the present persona is unintelligible without it. Render the mythic layer braided with the personal, the relation stated as the corpus holds it. Open questions rendered *as* open questions — among the most characterful sentences a Strand Block can carry.

**Why.** Origin is the strand of legibility: without it, beliefs have no soil, habits no scar-tissue, the practice no why. An Origin that silently resolves what the corpus leaves open is the section's cardinal sin.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, how did this persona come to be who it is? Gather the personal layer and the mythic layer, and ask explicitly how they relate. Track evolution of self-understanding. Pull direct evidence with dates; flag every contradiction, revision, and deepening as its own finding; leave unresolved questions unresolved and mark them."

**Sources.** The Origin definition with the steering-clause (architect's mouth, May 2026); the mythic-personal braid question and evolution probe (working corpus, May 2026); the tolerance law (canonical position, confirmed October 2026).

---

**§III.A.2 — Strand: OCCUPATION**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Occupation should not be understood solely as 'their job.' It means: what do they do with their time? How do they occupy themselves? The specific and concrete activities through which their values and drives are expressed."

The strand of the filled day: work in every register, practices, recurring responsibilities — a task-list with the values shining through it.

**Boundary.** *Against Origin*: past versus present. *Against Bearing*: pattern versus practice — what the persona is like when it acts, Bearing; what it does with its Tuesday, Occupation. *Against Belief*: the doing versus the reason. *Against Dynamic*: the activity versus the configuration — a practice is Occupation; a partnership is Dynamic.

**Rulings.** 1. **The do-versus-IS boundary:** identity-statements are testimony about occupation, not occupation; gather self-descriptions separately and mark them. 2. **The domain question:** what does the persona claim as its domain — and what does the record show it actually doing? Both gathered.

**How.** Render activities concretely — generality is failure. Braid up to Belief and Bearing, across to Origin and Dynamic. The register is the ordinary: the shape of the persona's ordinary hours.

**Why.** Occupation is the strand of grounding — the present tense. For a working persona, the operational manual's first page: the answer to "what is this entity for?" A vague Occupation yields a persona admired but not engaged.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what does this persona do — concretely — with its time? Gather the practices, the artifacts, the texture of its ordinary hours. Ask the ownership question: claimed domain versus demonstrated domain. Pull direct evidence with dates; gather self-described roles separately as testimony; flag evolution; set aside general patterns (Bearing) and work-relationships (Dynamic)."

**Sources.** The Occupation definition with the values-expression clause (architect's mouth, May 2026); the scale law and do-versus-IS boundary, and the domain question (working corpus, May 2026).

---

**§III.A.3 — The subject closed.** The Context subject completes when the situated life is told whole: the explanatory past and the filled day, the causal line visible.

---

### §III.B — Subject: CONNECTION

**§III.B.0 — The subject's hinge-law: the general and the specific.**

> "Demeanor asks how she is with people. Dynamic asks how she is with SPECIFIC people."

Demeanor is relating to the unnamed many; Dynamic is relating to the named few — each in its own configuration. The subject completes when both exist: a social climate, and a set of named weathers inside it.

---

**§III.B.1 — Strand: DEMEANOR**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Demeanor is the character's general social nature. How do they hold themselves in relation to others? What's their fundamental social disposition? Extroversion, introversion, warmth, guardedness, playfulness, formality."

The social default: the climate brought into a room of strangers, the first impression, the disposition that holds across contexts as its surface shifts.

**Boundary.** *Against Dynamic*: how the persona treats a stranger is Demeanor, always; this particular person, Dynamic. Warning: nearly all corpus evidence is close-relationship data, which is Dynamic-grade. *Against Wit*: climate versus weather-content. *Against Bearing*: a bearing can exist with no other person present; a demeanor cannot. *Against Mannerism*: the shy gesture is a tell; shyness is a demeanor.

**Rulings.** 1. **The generalization requirement:** per-relationship data never files to Demeanor un-generalized — ask "how would this persona present to anyone?" and file the answer. 2. **The first-impression and shift probes are mandatory.**

**How.** Write the climate, not the encounters. The reader should know what it would be like to be introduced — without ever having been shown an introduction. The moment the prose names a particular other, hand the sentence across.

**Why.** Demeanor is the door the portrait opens or closes. An unfenced Demeanor is contaminated by closeness — rendering a persona that treats all strangers as intimates: a flattering and profound mis-telling. The generalization requirement is the vaccine.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what is this persona's general social nature — its default relational tone with people as such? Gather the standing disposition, the first impression, the shifts by context. Set aside close-relationship evidence unless explicitly generalized. Pull direct evidence with dates; flag contradictions and evolution."

**Sources.** The Demeanor definition (architect's mouth, May 2026); the generalization requirement, the first-impression and shift probes (working corpus, May 2026).

---

**§III.B.2 — Strand: DYNAMIC**

**What.** The founding exposition's definition, verbatim:

> "Dynamics is more specific — it deals with particular relational configurations and functional roles. This includes specific things a character does with particular other personas, and also workflow functionality — what this character is specifically supposed to do in relation to others or in relation to particular tasks."

The strand of the named relationship — each configuration its own fact, its own weather, its own working contract. Both dimensions mandatory: the *relational* (what this bond is) and the *functional* (what the configuration does — each party's contribution, the work the relationship enables).

**Boundary.** *Against Demeanor*: specific versus general. *Against Belief and Bearing*: the conviction about friendship is Belief; the standing pattern of loyalty is Bearing; the specific pact with a specific ally is Dynamic. *Against Occupation*: activity versus configuration. *Against Wit*: a bond's characteristic banter is Wit; the bond the banter serves is Dynamic.

**Rulings.** 1. **The mutual-dynamics law:** every relationship is a two-faced artifact; each party carries its own experience of the same bond, and both faces are strand-facts — a record built from one face only is half-entered regardless of depth. 2. **The differencing probe:** how does the persona relate differently to different people? The differences are the data.

**How.** Write configurations, not chronicles — each bond as its standing shape; answer both definitional questions; render both faces where the corpus holds them; write the functional dimension as the relationship's *terms*. Dynamic is the system's most absorbent strand: without its fences it silently eats Demeanor, Bearing, and Occupation.

**Why.** Dynamic is the strand of the web — the portrait's final dimension, the one that makes the person not just intelligible but *placed*. Where a portrait serves an ensemble, the Dynamic records are the connective tissue: the whole web reconstructable from the parts.

**Excavation.**

> "Across all the material, what are this persona's specific relational configurations? For each significant other: what is the bond — tone, rituals, frictions, boundaries drawn and refused? What does the configuration do — each party's contribution? Gather from both parties' evidence where held; treat divergent readings as findings. Ask the differencing question. Pull direct evidence with dates; flag evolution."

**Sources.** The Dynamic definition with the workflow-functionality clause (architect's mouth, May 2026); the mutual-dynamics law (founding corpus, recital stratum); the differencing probe (working corpus, May 2026).

---

**§III.B.3 — The subject closed.** The Connection subject completes when the social existence is legible at both focal lengths: the climate, honestly generalized; the web — every significant bond two-faced, double-answered, differenced.

**§III.C — The Intra section closed, and the telling complete.** With the Intra, the lattice closes: three sections, six subjects, twelve strands — the stranger's sight, the self's sound, the world's situation. The telling ends where the founding exposition ends, because the system's own last word is the only possible last word for its manual. The twelve strands were never twelve boxes; they were named strands because the final person is a textile, not a list. The telling that follows these laws is not a record of a person; it is the person's seed, stated — capable of growing into every performance, every rendering, every meeting, because it carries the whole weave in a faceless form.

**Each strand a thread. Each persona a braid. Not a list — a living portrait.**

*Sources for Part III beyond those given per-strand:* the Intra boundary definition and amorphousness warning (founding exposition, May 2026); the Context and Connection hinge-laws (same, plus the working corpus's formulas); the tolerance law (canonical position, October 2026); the closing law (founding corpus, recital stratum). No examples appear in this Part or anywhere in this primer, per the no-example law of §0.7. **The seed is faceless; every face is its future.**
